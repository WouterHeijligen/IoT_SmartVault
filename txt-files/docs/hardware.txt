## OLED connections (SPI1)

The SSD1306 is a six-pin SPI module without a CS connection:

OLED label | Pi signal | Physical header pin

VCC | 3.3 V | 1 or 17 (3.3 V rail)

GND | Ground | For example 6 (common ground rail)

RES / RST | GPIO 16 | 36

DC | GPIO 6 | 31

SDA / DIN (data) | GPIO 20 / MOSI1 | 38

SCL / CLK (clock) | GPIO 21 / SCLK1 | 40

The OLED uses no MISO wire. Its SDA and SCL labels refer to SPI data and clock, not I2C. The MCP3008 uses SPI0 (GPIO 11/10/9, CS 25).

Display() opens SPI1 with busio.SPI(clock=board.D21, MOSI=board.D20). The application, root preflight and standalone display test use this same default. Display.close() releases a bus created by the display; an explicitly supplied SPI bus remains owned by its caller. SPI1 configuration is described in the setup notes (setup.html#spi1).

## Pico 2 W perimeter node

A separate Raspberry Pi Pico 2 W runs CircuitPython and measures distance with an RCWL-9610A sonar in trigger/echo mode. It publishes the distance in cm to the MQTT broker on the Pi (topic vault/perimeter/distance). Numbers below are physical pin numbers of the Pico. Firmware and install notes are in pico_2w/.

Sonar pin | Pico 2 W pin

VCC | 36 (3V3 OUT)

GND | 38

TRIG | 32 (GP27)

ECHO | 34 (GP28)

On 3.3 V the echo signal is 3.3 V, so no level shifting is needed. If the sensor is powered from 5 V (pin 40), add a 1 kΩ / 2 kΩ divider on ECHO.

## Known diagram differences

- The updated MCP3008 chip-select connection uses GPIO 25, matching the code. The central SPI bus list still mentions GPIO 8 / CE0.

- The OLED edges show GPIO 20 and 21, matching the current connections. However, GPIO 21 is SCLK1, not MISO. The OLED module's text incorrectly mentions MOSI and MISO; it uses MOSI and SCLK, without a MISO wire. Its 0x3C label is an I2C address, not an SPI address.

- The BH1750 SCL edge says GPIO 11 but also physical pin 5. Physical pin 5 is GPIO 3 / SCL1, matching sensors.py; GPIO 11 is the ADC's SPI0 clock. Do not wire BH1750 SCL to GPIO 11.

- The diagram uses GPIO 19 for the blue LED; the code uses GPIO 26.

- The Pico section of the diagram shows an HC-SR04 and may use other pins; the Pico firmware in pico_2w/ uses an RCWL-9610A on GP27 / GP28 as listed above.

## Electrical checks still required

Use 3.3 V-compatible GPIO signals and verify the MCP3008 supply/reference, sensor supply requirements, common ground, and LED resistors against the actual components. In particular, verify the diagram's 3.3 V LM35 supply label against the exact sensor's rated supply range. Motors must use a suitable driver, not GPIO power. The Pico sonar is powered from 3.3 V; at 5 V its echo signal needs a voltage divider.
