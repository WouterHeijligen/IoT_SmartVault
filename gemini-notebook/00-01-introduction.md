# Presentations 0 + 1 — Course and IoT introduction

**Sources:** `0_Course_Introduction_WT_v2.pptx` and
`1_Introduction_to_IoT_v2.pptx`.

## Video prompt

```text
Create an engaging 8–10 minute exam-preparation video overview using only
Presentations 0 and 1 as sources. Treat these as one introductory chapter.

Explain the course context and required knowledge briefly, then teach what IoT
means and why IoT is about creating value rather than merely connecting devices.
Walk through the Information-Value Loop in order: sensing/creating data,
communicating it, aggregating it, analyzing it, and acting on the result.
Explain the role of sensors, communication protocols, data platforms, analytics
and intelligence, and actuators. Use the toothbrush-to-smartphone and
truck-tracking examples to show how data becomes useful action. Explain the
risk-related and time-related value drivers, and discuss the off-grid camera
trap question as a boundary case.

Use the Raspberry Pi and Pico examples only to orient the learner; do not turn
the video into a board setup tutorial. Define technical terms on first use,
connect each example back to the value loop, and distinguish a device or
component from the complete IoT solution. End with a concise concept map,
five likely oral-exam questions, and a short recap.

Stay faithful to the slides. Cite the source presentation and slide or section
for important claims. If a detail is not in the sources, identify it as an
open question rather than filling it in from memory.
```

## Extensive quiz prompt

```text
Build an exam-level question bank of exactly 45 questions (never exceed the
Notebook limit of 50) for the combined introductory chapter in Presentations
0 and 1. Use only those presentations.

Progress from foundational recall to application:
- 8 terminology or concept questions;
- 12 explain/compare questions about IoT, the Information-Value Loop, and its
  components;
- 15 scenario questions that ask the learner to map an application to the loop
  and explain where value is created;
- 10 challenging questions about value drivers, the examples, or whether a
  boundary case qualifies as IoT.

Use a mix of multiple choice, short answer, ordering, and oral-exam prompts.
Make distractors plausible and explain why they are wrong. Do not make
questions depend on Raspberry Pi configuration details from later chapters.

First show all questions without answers. Then provide a clearly separated
answer key with concise reasoning and a source presentation plus slide/section
for each answer. Mark any answer that is an interpretation rather than an
explicit statement in the slides. Avoid repeating the same fact in different
wording.

After the question bank, include an optional interactive mode: ask one question
at a time, wait for my answer, give feedback, track missed concepts, and finish
with a short review of my weak areas.
```
